# The cure period is a staffing decision

Wrote 600 seconds into the parameter table this morning and then spent the
afternoon working out what that sentence commits somebody to.

Ten minutes from the margin call to the default declaration. Inside that: our
detection, our alert reaching a human, that human deciding, and a confirmed
transaction on chain. If alerting takes four minutes, six are left, and a person
opening a runbook they have never read will use all six.

So the number is not a risk parameter, it is a staffing requirement, and it
belongs in the operations page more than in the parameters page.

Considered longer. Twenty minutes is more humane and strictly worse: the position
keeps moving, and the loss the waterfall has to absorb grows with the clock.
Considered shorter. Five minutes turns every call into a default, which converts
a manageable event into the expensive one.

Ten is not derived from anything. It is the number where both bad outcomes are
survivable, and I would rather write that down than pretend it fell out of a
model.
