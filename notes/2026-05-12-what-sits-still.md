# What actually sits still

Trying to size the problem before writing anything about it. The claim we would
be making is that prefunding is expensive, and I wanted a number rather than an
adjective.

Took the simplest case I could think of. A maker quoting two sides of one asset
on one venue has to hold inventory and cash simultaneously, because either side
of the quote could be hit. Two venues doubles it, because the inventory cannot be
in two accounts at once. Five assets multiplies it again.

So the capital requirement is not the maker's net risk, it is the cartesian
product of the places where it might be asked to deliver. That is the whole
observation, and it took an embarrassing amount of paper to get to.

The counter-argument, which is fair: makers already solve this with inventory
management and by simply not quoting everything everywhere. True, and that is the
cost, paid as spread and as absent quotes rather than as a line in a balance
sheet. Nobody sees it, which is why nobody complains about it.

What I cannot do yet is put a number on the chain as it actually is. Volumes are
thin enough that a measurement today would say more about the current market than
about the mechanism.
