# The argument about the concentration cap

Twenty-five percent of a member's limit in one asset. This is the parameter that
will get pushed back on hardest, so I want the reasoning written down before
somebody asks.

The limit comes from a haircut, and the haircut assumes the position can be
auctioned near its mark. That assumption is fine for a diversified book and false
for a concentrated one. A quarter of a member's book in one thinly traded
tokenised equity cannot be auctioned at anything like its mark, and the number
the limit was derived from stops meaning what it said.

The objection is reasonable: a specialist market maker in one name is being told
to hold collateral as if it were a generalist. True. It is also true that the
mutualised fund of the other members is what absorbs the difference when the
auction disappoints, and those members did not choose that specialism.

Two ways out, both worse. Per-member caps negotiated individually, which is
discretion with extra steps. Or a higher tier for concentrated positions, which
prices the same risk twice.

Keeping the flat cap and writing the reasoning into the collateral page. If it
turns out to be too tight in practice, that is a governance proposal with 48
hours of notice, not a phone call.
